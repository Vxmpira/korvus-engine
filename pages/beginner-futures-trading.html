Beginner futures trading

Why is the market moving? The question every beginner asks, answered in plain terms.

ES drops six points in a minute. NQ rips while you were refilling your coffee. Every beginner in index futures has stared at a candle and asked the same thing: what just happened? This page explains where those moves come from, and how traders find out fast.

Price does not move on its own

Index futures like ES (S&P 500) and NQ (Nasdaq-100) track expectations about the whole market. When those expectations change, price changes. The usual suspects:

  • Scheduled data. CPI, jobs numbers, FOMC decisions. The calendar is public; the result is not. Minutes around these releases are the fastest of the day.
  • Headlines. A tariff announcement, an earnings warning from a mega cap, a geopolitical shock. Unscheduled, and the market reprices in seconds.
  • Fed speakers. A single sentence about rates can move every index at once.
  • Session mechanics. The open, the close, option expirations, and overnight sessions each have their own behavior.

The beginner trap: trading the candle, not the cause

New traders see a sharp move and jump in, long because it is going up or short because it is falling. Without knowing the cause, that is a coin flip. A move driven by a hot CPI print behaves nothing like a move driven by one stock's earnings, and neither behaves like a quiet session drifting on no news at all.

Knowing why price is moving does not tell you where it goes next. Nothing does. But it tells you what kind of move you are looking at, and that is the difference between a decision and a guess.
