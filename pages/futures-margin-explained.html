Futures margin explained for index traders

Margin is not a discount and it is not a loan. It is the deposit your broker holds against an open futures position. Here is what the terms mean, why the numbers move, and what to check before you size a trade.

Initial margin to open Maintenance margin to hold Variation daily mark to market

The two numbers that matter

Initial margin is the amount required to open a futures contract. Maintenance margin is the lower floor your account must stay above while the position is open. If equity drops below maintenance, your broker can issue a margin call and ask for more funds.

Both figures are set by the exchange and adjusted by your broker. They are not fixed for life. Read the current requirement in your broker's contract specs before every session, not from a number you saved months ago.

Why requirements change

Volatility

When the market moves faster, exchanges often raise the margin needed to carry the contract.

Contract and session

Requirements can differ by contract, by expiry, and by whether the position is held overnight.

Broker rules

Brokers can set requirements above the exchange minimum. Your broker's number is the one that binds you.

Daily settlement

Gains and losses post to your account every day. Your equity moves even when you do nothing.

A short checklist before you size

  • Confirm the current initial and maintenance figures with your broker.
  • Work out how far price can move against you before equity hits maintenance.
  • Keep spare funds above the floor, not just above the entry requirement.
  • Know what your broker does in a margin call and how fast.
