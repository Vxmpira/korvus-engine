ES and Index Futures Trading Hours and Sessions, Explained

E-mini S&P 500 (ES) futures do not trade in one block of time. They trade almost around the clock, but the character of the market changes hour by hour. This guide walks through the schedule, the daily halt, and the sessions that hand the market from one region to the next.

Two ways to trade ES: Globex and RTH

CME Group lists ES on its Globex electronic platform. Globex is the trading venue that stays open for most of the week. RTH, regular trading hours, is the core window when the US cash stock market is open and trading volume is usually heaviest.

Beginners often think "ES hours" means one time range. It is really two layers. Globex sets the outer boundary. RTH sits inside it and is where most of the day's volume and price discovery tends to happen.

The 9:30 ET open

The US stock market opens at 9:30 AM ET. That open is the moment when ES joins the cash market at full strength. Orders from stock traders, index funds, and large institutions tend to arrive together. Spreads often tighten and moves can become faster.

Many beginners treat the first half hour after the open as a separate world. Price can jump quickly as the overnight range meets live US participation. This guide does not say what to do with that. It only marks the time so you can understand what you are looking at.

The overnight session

Between the 6:00 PM ET reopen and the 9:30 AM ET open, ES trades on Globex without the US cash market. This is often called the overnight or electronic session. Volume is usually thinner than RTH, so single orders can move price more than they would during the day. Liquidity can also change quickly when news breaks.

The character of overnight trading depends on who is active. Asian and European participants are more present. Economic releases from other regions can shift the tone. Overnight highs and lows are commonly watched because they show where price travelled while the US was away.

The daily halt

Globex closes for one hour each weekday evening for maintenance. In CT that is 4:00 to 5:00 PM. In ET it is 5:00 to 6:00 PM. During the halt no ES trades execute, and charts stop printing new prices. When the session reopens, the first prints can start from a different level than the last print before the halt.

The halt is not a holiday and it is not a bug. It is a scheduled break. Knowing it exists helps beginners avoid reading a gap on the chart as a market event when it is only the reopen after maintenance.

London and Asia: the session handoff

Futures are global, so the day is usually described as a chain of sessions. Asia leads, Europe follows, and the US takes over. Each handoff can bring new volume and a new tone.

  • Asia session. Roughly the Tokyo and wider Asian business day, which falls in the late evening and early morning in ET. Activity is often lighter for ES, though regional news can matter.
  • London session. The European morning. London usually opens early in ET, and its activity often builds before the US arrives. Many traders watch how London trades relative to the overnight range.
  • US session. The cash open at 9:30 ET brings the largest concentration of participants and the most news-driven moves.

These session names are approximate windows, not sharp switches. Exact start and end times depend on local clock changes, which do not all happen on the same date.

The daylight saving shift

US daylight saving time ends on November 1, 2026. After that date US clocks move back one hour. CT and ET move together, so the one hour gap between them stays the same. The ET times in this guide will still read the same on your clock, but their position against UTC and against London changes.

London changes its own clocks on a different schedule. For a few weeks each year the gap between US and European hours is not what it usually is. If you compare times across regions, check each city's current offset rather than relying on a fixed rule.

A beginner checklist

Know before you look
  • Which clock your chart uses, CT or ET.
  • When the daily halt starts and ends.
  • Whether you are looking at Globex or RTH data.
  • Which contract month is front month.
Common confusions
  • A gap after the halt is a reopen, not a news event.
  • Thin overnight volume can exaggerate moves.
  • Session names are approximate, not exact switches.
  • Holiday schedules change the usual hours.
