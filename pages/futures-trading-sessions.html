Futures Trading Sessions Explained

Index futures do not sleep the way a stock exchange does. This guide walks through the Asia, London and New York sessions, when each one opens in Eastern Time, and what each one tends to look like.

Asia London New York All times in ET

What a session means

A trading session is a stretch of the day when a particular group of markets is most active. The label usually comes from the region where the largest share of trading is happening at that hour. The sessions overlap, so one ends while the next is already building.

Index futures such as the E-mini S&P 500 and Nasdaq contracts trade almost around the clock on CME Globex. The session labels do not mean the market closes between them. They describe who is most present and how the tape tends to move.

Session by session

Asia

Tokyo leads the Asian window. Roughly 7:00 PM to 4:00 AM ET, with Sydney opening a little earlier at about 5:00 PM ET. Volume in index futures is often lighter than later sessions. Ranges can be tighter, and price often moves within levels that were set the previous day.

London

The London session opens around 3:00 AM ET and runs to about noon ET. European cash markets bring in more participants, and this window often sees the first larger moves of the US day. Many traders watch how price behaves as London takes over from Asia.

New York

US trading runs from about 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM ET. The cash equity open at 9:30 AM ET is when many new data releases and participants arrive together. It is commonly the most active part of the day for US index futures.

What each tends to look like

These are general patterns that beginners often describe. They are not guarantees, and every day has its own character. Patterns repeat more loosely than textbooks suggest.

Asia often shows slower, range bound activity with smaller candles. London often shows the first directional push of the day, sometimes sweeping levels from the prior session. New York often brings the widest swings, especially around scheduled releases and the cash open.

Common beginner mixups

Treating a session as a clock

Sessions blur at the edges. A label is a rough guide to who is active, not a switch that flips at an exact minute.

Ignoring the calendar

Economic releases can dominate any session. Check the agenda for the day rather than assuming a quiet hour will stay quiet.

Confusing time zones

Local market times differ from ET. Convert every open to Eastern Time before comparing sessions, especially around daylight saving changes.
