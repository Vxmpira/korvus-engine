Premium and Discount, Explained Plainly

Premium and discount is a way to describe where price sits inside a range. It is a reference point. It is not a forecast.

The basic idea

Pick a range with a recent swing high at the top and a recent swing low at the bottom. Find the midpoint between them. That midpoint is often called equilibrium, or the 50 percent level.

Price above the midpoint sits in the premium zone. Price below it sits in the discount zone. That is the whole definition. Traders who use the framework talk about whether price is trading at a premium or a discount to the range they chose.

Drawing the range

  • Mark a swing high and a swing low on the timeframe you actually trade.
  • Draw the range from one extreme to the other.
  • Find the midpoint. Many charting tools will compute it for you.
  • Label the upper half as premium and the lower half as discount.

Two traders can draw different ranges on the same chart and get different zones. The choice of swings matters more than the arithmetic. Write down which swings you used so you can judge the idea later.

Why traders keep the midpoint in view

The midpoint gives a simple question to ask a chart. Is price currently cheap or expensive relative to the range you defined? Some traders use that context to frame what they are watching for. Others use it only to avoid chasing extremes.

Neither use turns premium or discount into a signal. A chart can sit in premium for hours. The zone describes location. It does not describe direction or outcome.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the zone as a buy or sell instruction. It is context only.
  • Changing the swing points after price moves, so the range always looks right.
  • Ignoring the timeframe. A range on a one minute chart and a daily chart tell different stories.
  • Stacking too many reference lines until the chart stops being readable.
